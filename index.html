02

Love That Is Harmed

I feel anger, and to be specific, frustration and disappointment: at how carelessly people act, and at the terrible consequences it brings for the climate and for nature.

If it keeps going, up to half of all species may face extinction by 2100. A mass extinction reaches our farms and our food too. And still, people choose not to help. What is crazy is that many are not even aware of what they are doing.

People are not interested. They don't talk about it. Everybody seems to miss the elephant in the room.

Up to 50% of species may face extinction by 2100.

Source: What is Missing?

It is the anger that comes from love that is harmed: love for my hometown, for Kyrchyn, for my country, for a small lake fifteen minutes from campus. For everything.
03

The Strongest Feeling

Sadness is the strongest. I know what is happening, but I feel I can't change it, or even influence it. That helplessness is what makes me sad.

We are the smartest species that has ever lived, and we have been more harmful to the planet than any species in the history of the Earth.
04

American Lake

Fifteen minutes from PLU.

Whenever I feel empty or sad, I go to American Lake. It is beautiful, and it brings joy to the whole community. On hot days people swim, fish, train, and simply enjoy it.

But people also leave trash there. Once I saw a drunk couple throw a glass bottle into the lake. I couldn't understand why anyone would do that. That glass will stay there for hundreds of years, maybe thousands, and it makes it so hard to keep our lake clean.

What hurt most was that they were a young couple: the next generation, the ones meant to keep the planet safe, healthy and clean. It is our duty to protect the Earth and nature. Seeing them care so little makes me sad.

hundreds of years · maybe thousands
05

The Diver

But I also feel empowered and inspired by people. My brother's friend was a wonderful diver. In his free time, he would dive deep into the lake to collect trash. It is hard work, even dangerous, and at first I never understood why he would risk his life for something so small.

But it was not small. He inspired people, and he inspired me. He made me see how powerful, and how positive, one person can be.

I want to be a person people look up to, someone who inspires them to do good.
06

Where My Feelings Go

Naming them is the first step. Then they become something.

My feelings go into action. My friends and I help clean up spaces, fields and parks. We read about it and learn about it, and we talk to people about it. That helps me feel better, because I see that I am not the only one who feels this way.

My practice

I am not the only one.
07

Connections to the Readings

Suzanne Crawford O'Brien, Climate Anxiety and Spiritual Resilience, Chapter 2: “Retrain your brain,” and the Climate Emotions Wheel

Chapter 2 · pp. 31–33

Feelings are data

Naming our feelings, and putting them into words, images, music or action, calms the brain's alarm. Our emotions are some of our best data: they show us what we truly care about.

My anger shows what I love: my hometown, Kyrchyn, my country, American Lake. It is the anger that comes from love that is harmed.

Chapter 2 · pp. 27–28

The elephant in the room

The chapter explains why people struggle to face climate change: we downplay how immediate the threat is, our brains misread the facts in predictable ways, and we want to believe we are good people in a community of good people.

I see this around me. People don't talk about it, and everybody seems to miss the elephant in the room. That is where my frustration comes from.

Chapter 2 · p. 24

From feeling to action

People who feel climate distress are more likely to take climate action. Grief and anxiety, when they are faced and given shape, become part of the work.

That is true for me. My feelings go into action: cleaning up with friends, reading, and talking to people. And the diver showed me how much one person can do.
